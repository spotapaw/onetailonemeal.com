Why technology

Kindness is everywhere.
Coordination isn’t.

In every city there are people who feed, rescue and rehome street animals. What they lack is a way to see where help is needed, find each other, and keep going without running out of money.

How the apps fund meals

Every purchase pays for
street meals

Every a-Paw app has a job in feeding street animals. The share is written down in advance, so it can be checked.

Spot a Paw

Free forever. It earns nothing; the paid apps pay for it.

Care, Track, Snap, Mind

About a quarter of every purchase pays for meals. Care Premium pays for 5 street meals every month (40 a year), Track Protect for 3 (25 a year), and a Snap pack for 4 or 10.

Adopt-a-Paw

50% of net revenue from shelter plans. Adopters never pay.

Feed-a-Paw

Sells nothing. Gifts made on this website go to the food truck operation.

One meal in Cairo costs about A$0.27 (10 Egyptian pounds). The meals come out of our revenue: a subscriber pays only the price of the app. Each year we will publish how many meals the apps funded.

How we use AI

AI does the slow part.
A person decides.

Rescuers are short of time, not of care. We use AI where it saves them minutes: turning a photo into a draft, spotting a duplicate, translating, planning a round. Three rules apply everywhere.

You stay in control

AI suggests; people check and choose. Nothing is posted, matched or sent on an AI’s say-so alone, and every suggestion can be corrected.

Areas, never addresses

Animals, feeders and homes are shown by area, not by exact spot. Colonies and people are safer when they can’t be pinpointed.

Honest labels

On these pages every feature says whether it works today, is coming soon, or is planned. We would rather under-promise.

Spot‑a‑Paw

  • TodayTurns a photo into a draft report: the animal and its visible needs. You check and correct it before posting.
  • TodayBefore a report goes up, looks for what may be the same animal reported nearby in the last three days.
  • TodaySearch in your own words: finds related reports even when the wording differs.
  • TodaySuggests feeding rounds from recorded feeding patterns, highlighting colonies that may be overdue.
  • TodayArabic and English side by side: community updates are translated next to the original.
  • TodayDrafts a shareable rescue story from a report's timeline, for you to review.

Feed‑a‑Paw

  • PlannedPlan truck routes and portions from Spot a Paw's feeding records, so meals go where they are most needed.

Adopt‑a‑Paw

Around the world

Started in Cairo and Sydney.
Built for any city.

Spot a Paw began on the streets of Cairo, in Arabic and English. Nothing about the apps is tied to one place: a map, a community, a round of feeding spots and a shelter work the same in Lisbon, Lagos or Bangkok.

  • Two languages today, more as communities ask for them.
  • One account works across every a-Paw app.
  • Local groups welcome. If you run a feeding or rescue group and want your city on the map, write to support@spot-a-paw.com.
