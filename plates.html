Plates you can eat

Nothing left behind but a full belly

When we feed street dogs and cats in Cairo, we don't want to leave plastic or paper on the street. So we are building plates made from vegetables: rescued, pressed and baked into a plate the animal can eat once the meal is finished.

Our vision

From rescued vegetables to a plate they can eat

This is the plan. Nothing is running yet, and every picture on this page is an AI concept until the first test bake gives us real photos.

AI concept
1

Rescue

Markets and farms throw away vegetables that are misshapen, too small or unsold. The plan is to collect them before they go to waste.

AI concept
2

Press

The vegetables are cooked, mashed and pressed in a heated plate press into thin, round plates.

AI concept
3

Bake

The plates are baked until they are dry and strong enough to hold a meal.

AI concept
4

Serve

Each animal gets its meal on a plate it can eat too. Nothing is left on the street.

Can it really be done?

Yes. Plates made from vegetables already exist. Teams in New Zealand, Belgium and Argentina have pressed plates and trays from vegetable starch and vegetable waste. We are adapting the idea into a recipe that is safe for dogs and cats, and that we can make in Cairo.

Safe for animals

We will only use vegetables that are safe for dogs and cats, fully cooked and peeled, with no salt, onion or garlic.

What happens next

Test it, show it, then scale it

Test bake

We will make our first test plates and try them with real meals.

Share the results

We will post the video, whether they hold up or fall apart.

Scale up

Once the recipe works, the plan is to press plates for every truck run.
