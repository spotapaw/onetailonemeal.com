The truck

Turning the plan into wheels

A road-legal van with a food-grade mixing drum on the back: it collects, cooks, drives and serves, then washes itself down and does it again tomorrow.

How it works

Four things the truck has to do well

Mix and cook on board

A rotating food-grade drum mixes and heats fresh ingredients safely, so meals leave the truck hot and clean.

Store it properly

Sealed ingredient bins, cold storage and a separate raw area, so nothing spoils on a Cairo afternoon.

Serve where they are

Meals go out through a side hatch into trays, bowls or known feeding spots — the animals never have to come to us.

Leave nothing behind

Edible plates, sealed waste bins and a wash down at every stop. The street looks the same after we have been as before.

Inside the truck

What we are actually building

  • Vehicle and structure. A modified, road-approved van with the drum and storage securely mounted.
  • Preparation zones. Separate areas for raw ingredients, mixing, heating, washing and serving, so they never cross.
  • The drum. Food-grade, with optional heating and a controlled pour-out, so a portion goes into a tray rather than over a shoe.
  • Utilities and safety. Water tanks, lighting, power and non-slip flooring for whoever is working in there.
  • Hygiene and waste. Wash sinks, sealed waste bins and a sanitising routine between stops.
A day on the round

Park, mix, serve, clean, move on

Where we serve

Streets with large stray populations, and feeding spots that local feeders and NGOs already keep. We go where the need is known, not where it photographs well.

Who drives it

A paid local driver who cares about animals, with volunteers alongside. Paid, because a round that runs on goodwill alone stops the week someone gets sick.

What we publish

Meals served, stops made and the schedule — so anyone who gave us money can see what it bought.

Permits and rules

Done properly, or not at all

A truck handing out meat in a public street has to satisfy Egyptian law, and we would rather be slow than shut down. This is the list we are working through.

  • Register the organisation as an NGO or social enterprise.
  • License the vehicle, including approval for the modifications.
  • Animal-feed and hygiene sign-off with the NFSA and the Ministry of Agriculture, where it applies to us.
  • Municipal permission to feed in specific public areas.
  • Insurance covering volunteers, the public, the animals and the equipment.
  • Then launch, with the round published openly.
Where the money goes

Your $8,000, line by line
