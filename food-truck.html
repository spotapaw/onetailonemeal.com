The truck

Turning the plan
into wheels

We are developing our first feeding truck for Cairo.
The plan is a van with a food-grade cooking drum (Qidra) on the back: it collects, cooks, drives and serves.
The vehicle, equipment and recipes still need specialist review, testing and local permissions before the first round.

On the round

One truck. One round.
Every tail fed.

How it works

Four things the truck
has to do well

01

Cook on board

A food-grade drum (Qidra) will cook while the truck is parked. Cooking, holding and serving steps will be set through trials before we serve.

02

Keep it fresh

Sealed bins, cold storage and a separate raw area, so nothing spoils on a hot Cairo afternoon.

03

Serve where they are

Meals go out through a side hatch to the spots the animals already know. They never have to come to us.

04

Leave nothing behind

Food will be served on edible plates (a prototype we are testing), waste will go in sealed bins, and every stop will be washed down.

Inside the truck

What we actually
plan to build

  • Vehicle and structure. A roadworthy vehicle that will need its inspections and approvals before it goes out.
  • Preparation zones. Separate areas for raw ingredients, mixing, heating, washing and serving, so they never cross.
  • The drum (Qidra). A fixed food-grade vessel with internal mixing, inside an insulated drum (Qidra), with a controlled pour-out so a portion goes into a tray rather than over a shoe. Size and heat source will be set through trials.
  • Utilities and safety. Water tanks, lighting, power and non-slip flooring for whoever is working in there.
  • Hygiene and waste. Wash sinks, sealed waste bins and a sanitising routine between stops.
The drum (Qidra)

A Cairo qidra,
made for strays

For centuries Cairo has cooked ful slowly in big metal drums and sold it hot from carts.
Our drum (Qidra) will work the same way, for street dogs and cats.
We are assessing a fixed food-grade vessel with internal mixing, cooked while parked, then kept at a controlled temperature for transport and serving.
Capacity, heating method and times will be set through equipment selection and trials.

  1. 1

    Cook while parked

    At our base, with the engine off.
    Cooking times and temperatures will be set through recipe trials and specialist review.

  2. 2

    Heat off, secured

    The heat will be switched off and the equipment secured before the truck moves.
    Nothing cooks while it drives.

  3. 3

    Drive to the round

    The food will travel at a controlled temperature.
    Holding times will be set through trials.

  4. 4

    Serve and wash

    At each stop, meals will be served at a suitable temperature on our edible plates.
    Back at base, the drum (Qidra) will be cleaned and its temperatures logged.

Capacity, cooking and holding times will be set through trials, and we will share the results.
A qualified technician will fit and check the heating equipment, and we will confirm the permits that apply before we build.

A day on the round

Park, mix, serve,
clean, move on

Where we will serve

Streets with large stray populations, and feeding spots that local feeders and NGOs already keep. We will go where the need is known, not where it photographs well.

Who drives it

A paid local driver who cares about animals, with volunteers alongside. Paid, because a round that runs on goodwill alone stops the week someone gets sick.

What we publish

Meals served, stops made and the schedule — so anyone who gave us money can see what it bought.

Plates they can eat

Nothing left behind
but a full belly

When we start feeding street dogs and cats in Cairo, we don't want to leave plastic or paper behind. So we plan to make plates from vegetables: rescued, pressed and baked into a plate the animal can eat once the meal is finished.

  • The plan is to use misshapen and surplus vegetables that markets and farms would otherwise throw away.
  • Which vegetables we use, how strong the plates are and how long they keep will all be tested first.
  • The aim is a plate the animal can eat, so there’s nothing to clean up.
