The plan

A simple idea that can help
thousands of tails

Every day, Cairo’s butchers are left with surplus meat they cannot sell. Every day, the animals a few streets away go hungry. The truck closes that gap.

1

Collect

Each morning the truck will collect accepted, traceable surplus meat from partner butchers.

Today: butchers can post leftover meat in the app for feeders and drivers to collect.Spot a Paw
2

Cook

It will be cooked on board in a food-grade drum (Qidra) while the truck is parked.
Recipes, cooking and serving steps will be tested and reviewed before the first round.

3

Drive

A local driver who knows the animals will run the same round every day: streets, alleys, markets and the quiet corners where colonies live.

Today: plan feeding rounds in the apps and mark each colony as fed, so other helpers know it’s covered.Spot a PawaPaw Family
4

Serve

Meals will go out on edible plates pressed from vegetables, a prototype we are still testing.
The aim is to leave nothing on the street behind us.

Edible plates

The plate is part of
the meal

We plan to serve meals on plates pressed and baked from rescued vegetables that markets would otherwise throw away. The aim: the animal eats the meal, then the plate.

  • Made from vegetables that would have gone to waste
  • Never onion, garlic or leek, which harm dogs and cats
  • No plastic and no litter left on the street
  • A prototype we will test for strength, hygiene and storage first

The truck

A kitchen on wheels,
planned for animals

The plan is a small van with a food-grade cooking drum (Qidra) on the back. The vehicle, equipment and recipes still need specialist review, testing and local permissions before the first round. Sealed bins for ingredients, cold storage, a cooker, a wash sink and a serving hatch, all in a vehicle small enough for Cairo’s back streets.

The drum’s design comes from the Egyptian qidra, the big pot Cairo’s street vendors use to slow-cook ful, the beans the city eats for breakfast. A shape every Cairo street already knows, reworked to feed its animals.

Every detail, from the hygiene routine to the permits we are assessing, is set out on the truck page.
