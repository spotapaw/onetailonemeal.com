The plan

A simple idea that can help
thousands of tails

Every day, Cairo’s butchers throw away meat and bone that is perfectly good for animals. Every day, the animals a few streets away go hungry. The truck closes that gap.

1

Collect

Each morning the truck will stop at partner butchers for the chicken, beef and bone they would otherwise throw away.

Today: In Spot-a-Paw, foster & grow communities of local butchers who create meat offers for collection by feeders and drivers.

2

Cook

It will be cooked on board in a sealed food-grade drum.
What reaches an animal will be hot, clean and safe, never raw scraps off the street.

AI concept
3

Drive

A local driver who knows the animals will run the same round every day: streets, alleys, markets and the quiet corners where colonies live.

Today: use Spot-a-Paw and the a-Paw Family app to create routes in your area.
Feed colonies and notify rescuers, feeders & drivers that they have been fed for the day.

4

Serve

Meals will go out on plates pressed from vegetables.
The animal eats the plate too, so nothing will be left on the street behind us.

Edible plates

The plate is part of
the meal

Every meal will be served on a plate pressed and baked from rescued vegetables that markets would otherwise throw away. The animal eats the meal, then the plate.

  • Made from vegetables that would have gone to waste
  • Never onion, garlic or leek, which harm dogs and cats
  • No plastic and no litter left on the street

The truck

A kitchen on wheels,
planned for animals

The plan is a road-legal, health-approved van with a food-grade cooking drum on the back. Sealed bins for ingredients, cold storage, a cooker, a wash sink and a serving hatch, all in a vehicle small enough for Cairo’s back streets.

Every detail, from the hygiene routine to the permits we need, is set out on the truck page.

Where you come in

We have taken this as far as
our own pockets go

To build the first truck, license it, and pay a local animal lover to drive it every day, we need people who believe compassion should not stop at a border.

Every donation, whatever the size, becomes a full belly, a wagging tail, and one more animal who learns that a human hand can mean something good.
